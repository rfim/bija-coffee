# Evening pilot: should Bija open one evening a week?

*Sprint 2 · Business Development · draft for Bija to approve · 29 Oct 2026*

**Recommendation: yes, test it in two stages.**

1. **Stage A (Jan–Feb 2027), alcohol-free.** Run *Malam Bija*, an Indonesian dessert and coffee night, on six Thursdays from 18:00 to 21:30. It closes before 23:00 and sells no alcohol, so **we expect no new licence to be needed**. Check the lease and planning first (section 2).
2. **Stage B, only if Stage A passes.** Add **coffee cocktails** for a few evenings under **Temporary Event Notices (TENs)**. Apply to change the premises licence only if those evenings also work.

This follows Grind's café-by-day, bar-by-night model. Its founder says adding cocktails "transformed the financials" ([Shopify case study](https://www.shopify.com/blog/grind-dtc-expansion); [Elite Network profile](https://www.elite-network.com/news/david-abrahamovitch-ceo-grind)). It also tests demand before Bija takes on any licensing risk in the West End.

> Numbers marked *(assumption)* have no source. The break-even model is on the *Evening break-even* sheet in [`bizdev-models.xlsx`](bizdev-models.xlsx). **Licensing, planning and VAT points must be confirmed with a licensing solicitor and an accountant.** This page summarises the process. It is not legal advice.

## 2. Licensing in Westminster

### 2a. Stage A: alcohol-free, closing before 23:00
- Under the Licensing Act 2003, a café needs a licence only for **alcohol**, **regulated entertainment**, or **late night refreshment** (hot food or drink between **23:00 and 05:00**). Closing at 21:30 with no alcohol and only background music should need **no licence**. **Confirm with a licensing solicitor**, including any conditions on an existing premises licence if Bija holds one.
- **Lease and landlord.** St James's Market is managed by The Crown Estate ([thecrownestate.co.uk](https://www.thecrownestate.co.uk/our-places/st-jamess)). Check the lease for permitted hours, service charges for out-of-hours opening, and any landlord consent.
- **Planning.** A café usually falls under Use Class E. Evening opening hours may be limited by planning conditions on the unit. **Check the planning history with Westminster planning or a solicitor.**

### 2b. Stage B: coffee cocktails
Selling alcohol needs authorisation. There are three routes.

**Route 1: Temporary Event Notice (TEN). Recommended for testing.**
- Westminster's TEN page: [Apply for a temporary event notice](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/licensing/alcohol-entertainment-and-late-night-refreshment-licences/apply-temporary-event-notice). Westminster must **receive** the notice **at least 10 working days** before the event. A *late TEN* can be given 5–9 working days before, but if anyone objects to a late TEN, the event cannot go ahead.
- **Fee: £21 per TEN** ([Westminster licensing fees 2026](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/media/document/licensing-fees-2026)).
- **Limits:** **15 TENs per premises per calendar year, 21 days in total**, and fewer than 500 people at a time ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/find-licences/temporary-events-notice); confirmed for 2024 onwards by [Enfield Council](https://www.enfield.gov.uk/services/business-and-licensing/temporary-event-notices)). A person **with a personal licence** can give 50 TENs a year. Without one, a person can give **5**.
- The police and Environmental Health can object within **3 working days**. Westminster then holds a hearing and may refuse ("counter notice").
- **What this means for Bija:** 15 TENs cover a weekly series of up to 15 weeks. If the owner or a manager gets a personal licence first, one person can give all of them.

**Route 2: personal licence (needed for routes 1 and 3).**
- Pass an accredited Level 2 Award for Personal Licence Holders *(assumption: course about £150–£250)*, plus a basic criminal record check, then apply to the council where you live. The statutory fee is **£37**. **Confirm with the council and a solicitor.**

**Route 3: new premises licence or full variation (only after the test).**
- Westminster's variation page: [Premises licence: variation applications](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/licensing/alcohol-entertainment-and-late-night-refreshment-licences/premises-licences/variation-applications). Adding alcohol sales needs a **full variation**. A [minor variation](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/licensing/alcohol-entertainment-and-late-night-refreshment-licences/premises-licences/minor-variation-applications) cannot add alcohol sales. If Bija has no premises licence at all, it needs a **new application** ([how to apply](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/licensing/alcohol-entertainment-and-late-night-refreshment-licences/premises-licences/how-apply)).
- **Process (Westminster):** submit the application, plans and fee. A blue notice goes up at the premises for **28 days** and an advert appears in a local newspaper within 10 working days. That starts a **28-day consultation**. With no representations, it is granted. If anyone makes a representation, a **Licensing Sub-Committee hearing within a further 28 days** decides.
- **Fee:** banded by rateable value, **£100 (Band A) to £635 (Band E)** for new and variation applications ([Westminster licensing fees 2026](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/media/document/licensing-fees-2026)).
- **West End Cumulative Impact Zone.** Westminster's [Statement of Licensing Policy](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/media/document/statement-of-licensing-policy---effective-october-2021) has a policy of refusing **new pubs and bars** in the West End CIZ, with limited exceptions. Bija would need to show alcohol is **ancillary to a café or restaurant**, kept within core hours, seated, with food. **Whether 2a St James's Market falls inside the CIZ, and which policy applies, must be confirmed with a Westminster licensing solicitor.** The current policy statement must also be checked, since we could only see the October 2021 version.
- **Realistic timeline:** 2–4 months, with a solicitor's fees on top *(assumption: £1,500–£4,000)*.

## 3. Staffing *(assumption)*

| Role | Stage A | Stage B |
|---|---|---|
| Barista / bar | 1 | 1 (a personal licence holder or someone they authorise; trained in Challenge 25 and refusals) |
| Floor, desserts, till | 1 | 1 |
| Hours | 17:30–22:00 (4.5 h) | Same |
| Rate | **£14.80/h**, the voluntary London Living Wage. The legal minimum for 21+ is **£12.71** from April 2026 ([Mondaq summary of the government announcement](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/employee-benefits-compensation/1772586/uk-government-increases-minimum-wage-rates-effective-april-2026-what-employers-need-to-know)) | Same |
| On-costs (employer NI, holiday, pension) | +15% | +15% |

Offer the evening first to existing staff as optional extra shifts. Don't make anyone close and then open. Keep a 10-hour gap between a late finish and the next shift.

| | Stage A | Stage B |
|---|---|---|
| Staff: 2 × 4.5 h × £14.80 × 1.15 | £153 | £153 |
| Utilities, cleaning, consumables *(assumption)* | £20 | £20 |
| Marketing *(assumption)* | £20 | £20 |
| TEN fee | – | £21 |
| **Fixed cost per evening** | **£193** | **£214** |
| Average spend per guest incl. VAT *(assumption)* | £12.00 | £16.00 |
| Net of 20% VAT (eat-in, standard-rated; **confirm with an accountant**) | £10.00 | £13.33 |
| Food and drink cost *(assumption)* | 30% | 28% |
| **Contribution per guest** | **£7.00** | **£9.60** |
| **Break-even guests per evening** | **28** | **23** |

This leaves out rent and rates, which Bija pays anyway. Every guest above break-even adds about £7 (Stage A) or £9.60 (Stage B). With 45 guests, Stage A makes about £120 a night, which is **about £6,200 a year from one evening a week** *(assumption)*.

## 5. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Too few guests after 18:00 (offices empty out) | Medium–high | Pre-sell tickets for the first two weeks. Ask Bija Card members. Pick Thursday, not Friday |
| Stage A doesn't need a licence, but **lease or planning** limits hours | Medium | Check before announcing (section 2a) |
| TEN objected to by police or Environmental Health | Low–medium | Seated, small, ends by 22:00. Neighbours told in advance. Start each series with a clean first evening |
| Premises licence refused in the CIZ | Medium | Don't apply until the TEN evenings prove demand. Use a specialist solicitor |
| Staff fatigue, morning shift suffers | Medium | Separate evening team. Track sick days and morning service times |
| Under-25s and refusals (Stage B) | Medium | Challenge 25 policy, refusals log, a personal licence holder on shift |
| Waste of desserts made for the night | Medium | Small batches. Sell leftovers next morning as "yesterday's cake" at a discount, if within shelf life |
| Brand: Bija known as a daytime café | Low | Keep it Indonesian, calm and seated. It's a dessert night, not a bar |

## 6. Go / no-go test

**Run Stage A on 6 consecutive Thursdays: 14 Jan to 18 Feb 2027.** Weeks 1–2 are the launch. Judge on weeks 3–6.

| Measure (weeks 3–6) | **GO** to Stage B | **ADJUST** (change day, menu or price, run 4 more) | **STOP** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average guests per evening | ≥ 35 | 25–34 | < 25 |
| Evenings at or above break-even (28) | 3 or 4 of 4 | 2 of 4 | 0–1 of 4 |
| Average spend per guest | ≥ £12 | £10–£12 | < £10 |
| Repeat guests (Bija Card scans) | ≥ 20% | 10–20% | < 10% |
| Complaints from neighbours or landlord | 0 | 1, resolved | Any unresolved |
| Next-morning service time | No worse | – | Noticeably worse |

**If GO:** give TENs for a 6-week Stage B series (6 of the 15 TENs a year). Use the same thresholds with a break-even of 23. Talk to a licensing solicitor about a variation only if Stage B also passes.
